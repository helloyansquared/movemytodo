To Google Tasks

Creates a new Google Tasks list for each To Do list and adds your tasks in the same order. Works on Android, iPhone, Gmail and Google Calendar.

  • Lists
  • Steps → subtasks
  • Notes and links
  • Due dates
  • Completed tasks (marked done)
  • Repeat rules and reminder times are written into the notes (Google's API can't set them)

To Apple Reminders

On a Mac (anything from the last few years): download the import file and run one command. It adds a new Reminders list for each To Do list, and iCloud syncs them to your iPhone and iPad.

  • Lists
  • Notes and links
  • Due dates and reminder times
  • Important → flagged, high priority
  • Completed tasks (marked done)
  • Steps go into the notes as a checklist
  1. Open Terminal (press ⌘ Space, type Terminal, press Return).
  2. Paste this and press Return: 
    osascript -l JavaScript ~/Downloads/import-to-reminders.js
  3. If macOS asks whether Terminal may control Reminders, click OK. You'll see “Done: added … reminders”.

No Mac? Read how to get To Do lists into Reminders on an iPhone (free, with a few limits).

To Things 3

Open this page on the Mac, iPhone or iPad where Things is installed, then tap each button. Each To Do list becomes a Things project (big lists come in parts of 40).

  • Lists → projects
  • Steps → checklist items
  • Notes and links
  • Due dates → deadlines
  • Reminders → “when” with time
  • Categories → tags

Todoist, TickTick, Notion and others

  • Todoist (free): download one Todoist CSV per list below, then in Todoist open a new project → ⋯ → Import from CSV. Open tasks and steps (as sub-tasks) with due dates; Todoist takes up to 300 tasks per file.
  • TickTick has its own Microsoft To Do importer in its web app (Settings → Integrations & Import), so you don't need us for that.
  • Notion, Airtable, Excel, Google Sheets: use the free CSV backup above. In Notion, choose Import → CSV.
  • Obsidian or any notes app: use the free Markdown backup.

What's happening

Microsoft To Do is shutting down

Microsoft is retiring the Microsoft To Do apps on June 30, 2027, on the web, Windows, Mac, iPhone and Android. Your lists don't move anywhere by themselves: personal accounts get no replacement app, and work or school accounts are pointed to Planner, but existing To Do tasks are not copied into it.

Microsoft To Do retirement: key facts (checked 26 November 2026)
AnnouncedNovember 18, 2026, in a Microsoft Planner blog post and the To Do transition guide.
Shutdown dateJune 30, 2027. The To Do apps stop working on web, Windows, Mac, iOS and Android.
Personal accountsOutlook.com, Hotmail, Live and Microsoft 365 Personal or Family get no replacement. Microsoft says to choose another task app.
Work or school accountsMoved to Planner (“My tasks” is being redesigned from December 2026). Existing To Do tasks and lists are not migrated into it; they stay visible in Planner until June 30, 2027.
Microsoft's exportPromised before the shutdown, but not available as of 26 November 2026. Microsoft hasn't said what format it will use, and says an export doesn't mean another app can import it.
Your dataRetiring the apps doesn't by itself schedule your task data for deletion, but there will be no app to see it in.
What reads To Do todayMicrosoft Graph, the official API that apps like MoveMyTodo use with your permission. It will also be retired later; no date yet.

Questions

Moving off Microsoft To Do

When is Microsoft To Do shutting down?

Microsoft To Do stops working on June 30, 2027, on the web, Windows, Mac, iPhone and Android. Microsoft announced it on November 18, 2026. Personal accounts get no replacement; work and school accounts are pointed to Planner.

Will my To Do tasks move to Planner or another app automatically?

No. Microsoft says existing To Do tasks and lists will not migrate to Planner, and personal accounts can't use Planner at all. You have to export or move them yourself before June 30, 2027.

How do I export Microsoft To Do?

Sign in to MoveMyTodo with your Microsoft account and download a free backup as CSV (opens in Excel and Google Sheets), Markdown, JSON or a printable page. Microsoft's own export isn't available yet as of 26 November 2026.

Can I move Microsoft To Do to Google Tasks?

Yes. MoveMyTodo signs in to Google Tasks with your permission and creates a list for each To Do list, with steps as subtasks, notes, due dates and completed tasks. Google Tasks has no import button of its own.

Can I move Microsoft To Do to Apple Reminders?

Yes, on a Mac: MoveMyTodo makes an import file that adds a Reminders list for each To Do list, with notes, due dates, reminder times and flags, and iCloud syncs it to your iPhone. Without a Mac, see the free iPhone method in our Apple Reminders guide.

Is it safe to sign in? What can MoveMyTodo see?

MoveMyTodo asks Microsoft for read-only access to your tasks, so it cannot change or delete anything in To Do. Your tasks are read straight into your browser tab and are never sent to or stored on our servers. Close the tab and they're gone.

Is MoveMyTodo free?

Scanning and the full backup (CSV, Markdown, JSON, printable) are free with no limits. Moving to Google Tasks, Apple Reminders or Things is free for up to 25 tasks from one list; MoveMyTodo Pro moves everything for $6.99 once.

Does it work with a work or school account?

Often, but not always. Some organisations only let you sign in to apps an admin has approved; if you see an admin-approval message, ask your IT team or use the free backup another way. Personal Microsoft accounts work straight away.

What happens to repeating tasks and reminders?

Due dates move everywhere. Google Tasks, Apple Reminders and Things can't receive a repeat rule through an import, so MoveMyTodo writes it into the task's notes, for example “Repeats: Every month on day 1”, and you set it again in the new app.

What about Todoist or TickTick?

TickTick has its own Microsoft To Do importer in its web app (Settings → Integrations & Import). For Todoist, MoveMyTodo makes free Todoist CSV files, one per list, that you import into a project. Either way, download the free backup first so you keep a copy you control.

Will moving delete anything from To Do?

No. MoveMyTodo only reads To Do, and in the new app it only adds new lists, never changing or deleting the lists you already have there.

What if I wait until after June 30, 2027?

The apps will be gone, and Microsoft hasn't promised how long the export or API will keep working after that. Back up now; it takes a minute and costs nothing.
