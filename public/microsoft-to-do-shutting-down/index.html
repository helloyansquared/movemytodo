Microsoft To Do is shutting down: what to do now

Microsoft To Do retires on June 30, 2027, on every platform. Personal accounts get no replacement and work or school accounts are pointed to Planner, but existing To Do tasks aren't moved anywhere. Back up your lists now, pick a new app, and move them while To Do still works.

Key facts

Microsoft To Do retirement: key facts (checked 26 November 2026)
AnnouncedNovember 18, 2026, in a Microsoft Planner blog post and the To Do transition guide.
Shutdown dateJune 30, 2027. The To Do apps stop working on web, Windows, Mac, iOS and Android.
Personal accountsOutlook.com, Hotmail, Live and Microsoft 365 Personal or Family get no replacement. Microsoft says to choose another task app.
Work or school accountsMoved to Planner (“My tasks” is being redesigned from December 2026). Existing To Do tasks and lists are not migrated into it; they stay visible in Planner until June 30, 2027.
Microsoft's exportPromised before the shutdown, but not available as of 26 November 2026. Microsoft hasn't said what format it will use, and says an export doesn't mean another app can import it.
Your dataRetiring the apps doesn't by itself schedule your task data for deletion, but there will be no app to see it in.
What reads To Do todayMicrosoft Graph, the official API that apps like MoveMyTodo use with your permission. It will also be retired later; no date yet.

What to do, in order

  1. Back up today. A free export takes a minute and protects you whatever happens to Microsoft's own export.
  2. Pick your new app. Google Tasks if you live in Gmail or Android; Apple Reminders if you're on iPhone and Mac; Todoist or TickTick for more power. See what moves to each.
  3. Move your lists. To Google Tasks, to Apple Reminders or to Things 3 with MoveMyTodo. Todoist and TickTick have their own free importers; see where each app stands.
  4. Set repeats and shared lists again. Repeating tasks keep their rule in the notes; shared lists need re-sharing in the new app.
  5. Keep To Do until you're happy. Nothing is deleted when you move; check the new app for a week, then stop using To Do.

Work and school accounts

Planner's new “My tasks” page starts rolling out in December 2026 and will hold new private tasks. Your old To Do tasks stay visible in Planner until June 30, 2027 but are not copied into it, so export anything you want to keep. If your organisation requires admin approval for apps, ask IT before using any third-party tool.

Questions

When is Microsoft To Do shutting down?

Microsoft To Do stops working on June 30, 2027, on the web, Windows, Mac, iPhone and Android. Microsoft announced it on November 18, 2026. Personal accounts get no replacement; work and school accounts are pointed to Planner.

Will my To Do tasks move to Planner or another app automatically?

No. Microsoft says existing To Do tasks and lists will not migrate to Planner, and personal accounts can't use Planner at all. You have to export or move them yourself before June 30, 2027.

What if I wait until after June 30, 2027?

The apps will be gone, and Microsoft hasn't promised how long the export or API will keep working after that. Back up now; it takes a minute and costs nothing.

What about Todoist or TickTick?

Both have their own free Microsoft To Do importers, so you don't need MoveMyTodo to get there. Todoist's importer (launched November 21, 2026) copies the lists you choose with due dates, notes and steps; TickTick's is in its web app under Settings → Integrations & Import. MoveMyTodo is for the apps without an importer: Google Tasks, Apple Reminders and Things 3. Either way, download the free backup first so you keep a copy you control.
