Microsoft To Do to Planner: what moves and what doesn't

Microsoft To Do tasks do not migrate to Microsoft Planner. Microsoft says work and school accounts can view existing To Do tasks in Planner until June 30, 2027, and not after. To keep them, download a backup of your lists and recreate the tasks you still need in Planner. Personal Microsoft accounts can't use Planner.

What Microsoft says happens

Microsoft To Do → Planner, work and school accounts (checked 26 November 2026)
Existing tasks and listsNot migrated. They stay visible and editable in Planner until June 30, 2027, then you can no longer view or manage them there.
New private tasksGo into a redesigned Planner “My tasks” page (a list view with stars and a separate “Assigned to me” view), rolling out from December 2026.
Shared listsMembership, assignments and permissions don't transfer.
RemindersPlanner sends due-date notifications, not To Do's per-task reminder times.
List groupsNo direct Planner equivalent.
File attachmentsNot supported on tasks in the new private Planner plan.
Desktop apps and offlineNo native Planner app for Windows or Mac is planned; Planner runs on the web and in Teams, without equivalent offline use.
AccessDepends on your organisation's subscription, your license and admin settings. If Planner is turned off, your admin has to enable it.
Classic Outlook TasksA separate feature; Microsoft says it keeps working and syncing with Exchange.

Source: Microsoft Support, “Moving from Microsoft To Do to Microsoft Planner”. Microsoft hasn't said whether My tasks will have steps, repeating tasks or notes like To Do.

How to keep your To Do lists

  1. Back up everything now. Sign in to MoveMyTodo with your work account (read-only) and download the free CSV, Markdown or JSON backup. If you see an admin-approval message, your organisation blocks third-party apps; ask IT, and wait for Microsoft's own export.
  2. Sort out what's still live. Open the CSV in Excel and filter to open tasks. Most people carry over far fewer tasks than they have.
  3. Recreate those tasks in Planner's My tasks. Microsoft hasn't published a way to import To Do lists into Planner, and MoveMyTodo can't write to Planner.
  4. Keep the backup. Completed tasks, notes and old lists stay readable in the file after June 30, 2027.

Personal tasks in a work account?

If you keep personal lists (groceries, travel) in your work account, consider moving those to a personal app instead, so they don't stay with your employer: Google Tasks, Apple Reminders, Things 3 or Todoist. If you use both a work and a personal Microsoft account, review each one separately.

Questions

Can I import Microsoft To Do into Planner?

No. Microsoft says existing To Do tasks and lists will not migrate to Planner, and it hasn't published an import. Work and school accounts can still see old To Do tasks in Planner until June 30, 2027. To keep them after that, download a backup (CSV, Markdown or JSON) and recreate the tasks you still need in Planner. MoveMyTodo can't write to Planner.

Can I still see my To Do tasks in Planner?

Yes, until June 30, 2027, for work and school accounts. Microsoft says you can view and manage existing To Do tasks in Planner until then, but not after. Personal Microsoft accounts can't use Planner at all.

Does it work with a work or school account?

Often, but not always. Some organisations only let you sign in to apps an admin has approved; if you see an admin-approval message, ask your IT team or use the free backup another way. Personal Microsoft accounts work straight away.

How do I export Microsoft To Do?

Sign in to MoveMyTodo with your Microsoft account and download a free backup as CSV (opens in Excel and Google Sheets), Markdown, JSON or a printable page. Microsoft's own export isn't available yet as of 26 November 2026.
