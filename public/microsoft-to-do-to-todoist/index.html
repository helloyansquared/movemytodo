How to move Microsoft To Do to Todoist

Todoist has its own free Microsoft To Do importer, launched November 21, 2026, and it works on every Todoist plan, including the free Beginner plan. You sign in to Microsoft with read-only access, choose lists, and each list becomes a Todoist project with its tasks, due dates, reminders and steps. On the free plan you can have 5 active personal projects.

Steps

  1. Back up first. Download a free backup of all your lists (CSV, Markdown or JSON), so you keep a copy that doesn't depend on either app.
  2. Open Todoist's Microsoft To Do importer and sign in to Todoist, or create a free account.
  3. Sign in with Microsoft. Todoist gets read-only access to your To Do lists.
  4. Choose one list to test, or several at once. Todoist shows how many new projects it needs before copying starts.
  5. Start the import. It runs in the background, usually takes a few minutes, and Todoist emails you a summary when it's done.

What carries over

Microsoft To Do → Todoist, according to Todoist (checked 26 November 2026)
ListsA new Todoist project for each list.
Tasks and stepsCopied as tasks, with steps.
Due dates and remindersCopied. Reminders over your plan's limits are skipped.
Repeating tasksCopied with their schedule when Todoist can match it; otherwise as a one-time task.
FilesCopied within your plan's file limits.
Shared listsCopied, but nobody is invited to Todoist; share the project again.
Your To Do listsNot changed. Todoist only reads them and disconnects when the import finishes.

Source: Todoist's Microsoft To Do importer page and Todoist's usage-limits help page. Importing the same list twice creates duplicate tasks.

Free plan limits to plan around

  • 5 active personal projects on the free Beginner plan (300 on Pro). With more than 5 To Do lists, merge small lists in To Do first, import your 5 most important lists, archive old projects, or upgrade.
  • 300 active tasks per project on every plan. Completed tasks don't count toward it.

If the importer doesn't fit

If your organisation blocks the Todoist app on your work account, or you'd rather not connect Todoist to your Microsoft account, MoveMyTodo makes a Todoist CSV file for each list, free. In Todoist, open a project, choose Import from CSV from the project menu, and pick the file.

Questions

Does Todoist's Microsoft To Do importer work on the free plan?

Yes. Todoist says its importer works on every plan, including the free Beginner plan. Each To Do list becomes a Todoist project, and the Beginner plan allows 5 active personal projects, so pick up to 5 lists, merge small ones first, or upgrade. Every Todoist plan allows 300 active tasks per project.

Do repeating tasks and reminders move to Todoist?

Mostly. Todoist's importer copies due dates, reminders and steps; a repeating task whose schedule Todoist can't match arrives as a one-time task, and reminders or files over your plan's limits are skipped. Todoist emails a summary of what changed.

What about Todoist or TickTick?

Both have their own free Microsoft To Do importers, so you don't need MoveMyTodo to get there. Todoist's importer (launched November 21, 2026, free on every plan) copies the lists you choose with due dates, reminders and steps; TickTick's is in its web app under Settings → Integrations & Import. MoveMyTodo is for the apps without an importer: Google Tasks, Apple Reminders and Things 3. Either way, download the free backup first so you keep a copy you control.

How do I export Microsoft To Do?

Sign in to MoveMyTodo with your Microsoft account and download a free backup as CSV (opens in Excel and Google Sheets), Markdown, JSON or a printable page. Microsoft's own export isn't available yet as of 26 November 2026.
